# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Michi is for adult beginners learning Japanese in short, focused sessions. Learners
need a clear route through unfamiliar material, honest feedback about what they can
recall, and a safe way back after time away.

## Product Purpose

Michi teaches durable, useful Japanese through a navigable learning world. Stations
introduce and test one manageable outcome; authored consolidation and spaced review
bring earlier knowledge back. Success means the learner can keep moving while building
real memory, not merely accumulating cosmetic completion.

## Brand Personality

Calm, crafted, trustworthy. Michi should feel purposeful and tactile, with the visual
confidence and legibility of Japanese transit wayfinding. Its world can be delightful,
but its controls should remain immediately understandable.

## Anti-references

- Childish or noisy language-learning interfaces.
- Streak pressure, depleted lives, punitive loss, and rewards for empty repetition.
- Generic game currencies, XP clutter, and attention-seeking celebration.
- Decorative or stereotyped “Japanese” styling without a functional reason.
- Inconsistent controls, ambiguous icons, low-contrast neumorphism, and one-off UI.

## Design Principles

1. **Wayfinding before chrome.** Every screen should make the learner's next useful
   action as clear as following a station sign.
2. **Learning before engagement.** Game mechanics must strengthen orientation,
   discovery, practice, or memory rather than manufacture obligation.
3. **Progress is permanently additive.** The world may open, collect, and celebrate;
   it never confiscates progress or punishes an absence.
4. **Evidence stays honest.** Completion, mastery, rehearsal, and review remain
   visibly distinct claims.
5. **One system, many stations.** Shared interaction rules should make new content feel
   authored within Michi rather than assembled from new controls each time.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, visible keyboard focus, reduced-motion alternatives, and
touch targets of at least 44 by 44 pixels. Meaning must never depend on color alone.
Audio actions require a recognizable speaker mark plus accessible labels and tooltips,
and every learning flow must remain usable when audio, pointer input, or motion is
unavailable.
